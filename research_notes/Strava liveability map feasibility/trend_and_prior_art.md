# Strava-as-liveability-proxy: the LinkedIn post, the 2026 trend, prior art, and critiques

Research date: 2026-10-17. All dates below are as stated by the source; older items are flagged with their year.

## Key Question 1: What does the LinkedIn post say, what source does it cite, and who is Cristiano Galvão?

### Takeaway
The post (c. mid-September 2026, 14 reactions) is a short commentary by Cristiano Galvão, a New York-based data-analytics instructor and Microsoft Excel MVP, re-sharing Jacob Swiney's 28 Aug 2026 Substack essay "Using Strava to Underwrite Apartment Investments". The post's framing is that Strava running heatmaps act as continuously updated "wealth maps" that lead lagging official data by 2-5 years, and that regressing heatmap intensity on census income and inspecting residuals can flag neighborhoods improving before official statistics show it.

### Cited Findings
- The LinkedIn page title is "Strava Heatmaps Reveal Promising Neighborhoods Before Prices Rise"; the author is Cristiano Galvao (35,323 followers); posted "1mo" before fetch (so roughly mid-September 2026); 14 reactions; it links via lnkd.in/gcQQj_SP to "a full article by Jacob Swiney"; no image/video attached; comments behind a login wall — [LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/cristianogalvao_people-are-using-strava-to-find-the-best-activity-7500762538289328128-QeY2)
- Post content (paraphrased from fetch): Strava running heatmaps are being discussed as "wealth maps" for desirable neighborhoods; heatmaps update continuously while official real-estate/urban datasets lag 2-5 years; Swiney's method extracts running routes from heatmap screenshots with Python, links them to geographic units, regresses against income, and inspects residuals for areas with strong running activity but low official income that "may be improving before official data shows it"; the post frames this as using behavioral data to detect early neighborhood change — [LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/cristianogalvao_people-are-using-strava-to-find-the-best-activity-7500762538289328128-QeY2)
- Cristiano Galvão (professional context only): LinkedIn profile lists Per Scholas (Bronx, NY) as current company; About section describes him as a "seasoned instructor and data analytics specialist"; Microsoft MVP seven times 2020-2026; speaker at Global Excel Summit 2026 (London) and NY Tech Week 2025 on Copilot, Python in Excel and data analysis; author of LinkedIn Learning Excel courses (Power Query, Power Pivot, VBA) 2019-2023; 2026 certifications include Power BI Data Analyst Associate and Google AI Professional Certificate; earlier roles in Rio de Janeiro — [LinkedIn profile](https://www.linkedin.com/in/cristianogalvao)
- The cited article: "Using Strava to Underwrite Apartment Investments", by Jacob on the *Chasing Basis* Substack, published 28 Aug 2026 — [Substack](https://cjacobswiney.substack.com/p/using-strava-to-underwrite-apartment)
- Swiney's thesis: investors use demand proxies (traffic counts, household income, net migration); the census has a "2-year lag"; Strava heatmaps identify where affluent, educated residents want to live — [Substack](https://cjacobswiney.substack.com/p/using-strava-to-underwrite-apartment)
- Swiney's supporting claims (no sources linked in the article): a 2015 World Triathlon Corporation survey put Ironman participants' average household income at ~$247,000 (~$348,000 in today's dollars, ~3x the US average he cites as $115,000); Running USA surveys found >76% of "core runners" are college graduates vs ~30% nationally — [Substack](https://cjacobswiney.substack.com/p/using-strava-to-underwrite-apartment)
- Swiney's method: heatmap screenshots for Midwest cities (Chicago, Indianapolis, Columbus, Cincinnati, Milwaukee, Detroit, St. Louis) and Tier-1 cities (LA, SF, DFW, Miami, NYC, Boston) shown visually only; a deep dive on Indianapolis / Marion County: a script reads the blue trail pixels, georeferences the image to town labels (fit "within about 290 meters, roughly a block"), scores ~8,000 grid cells of ~320 m, joins to 227 Marion County census tracts with 2023 ACS median household income, rent and population (free Census API key); the author says the pipeline can be built by prompting Claude — [Substack](https://cjacobswiney.substack.com/p/using-strava-to-underwrite-apartment)
- Swiney's result: regression of log income on log run intensity across 225 tracts gives R² = 0.25, t = 8.6, slope ≈ 13% higher median income per doubling of run intensity; a standardized "gap" map (running z-score minus income z-score) flags tracts where running exceeds income (red) or lags it (blue); Geist ($145.6K), Williams Creek ($193K) and Traders Point ($148K) are among the five largest negative gaps; some tracts with census income of $30-40K show heavy running — [Substack](https://cjacobswiney.substack.com/p/using-strava-to-underwrite-apartment)
- Swiney's own caveats: trail effects (Monon and Riverside trails inflate running intensity without demographic change); census lag; "can't tell which story is which from just looking at the map"; the mismatch map is a shortlist, not a conclusion; plans to recapture quarterly; no code repository is published — [Substack](https://cjacobswiney.substack.com/p/using-strava-to-underwrite-apartment)

### Inferences
- The LinkedIn post is derivative commentary on a single Substack essay rather than an original analysis; its "2-5 year lag" claim is stronger than Swiney's own "2-year lag" and is unsourced.
- Swiney's R² = 0.25 means running intensity explains only about a quarter of tract-level income variance in Indianapolis; the post's "wealth map" framing overstates the fit.
- The method relies on screenshot pixel-scraping of the Global Heatmap, which is fragile (georeferencing error ~290 m, login-walled high zoom levels, Strava's 2025-2026 anti-scraping changes; see KQ3/KQ4).

### Gaps
- Exact post date and comment thread are behind LinkedIn's login wall; only "1mo" and 14 reactions were visible.
- The lnkd.in short link was not resolved directly; the Swiney Substack is identified as the target from the post text ("full article by Jacob Swiney") and the matching title.
- Swiney's Ironman and Running USA statistics are unsourced in his article and were not independently verified here.

## Key Question 2: What is the "use Strava to pick where to live" trend — who covered it, and when?

### Takeaway
The 2026 wave started on X on 24 Aug 2026 with @NYCerInExile's Chicago heatmap post ("a giant proxy for class and wealth", 5.3M views), was amplified 25-26 Aug by @Terravoir ("Pro tip for moving to a new city: Use Strava as a proxy for where to live", 1.9M views) and others, and was picked up within days by ZME Science (28 Aug), Swiney's Substack (28 Aug), Domus (2 Sep), The Overhead Wire (Sep), Hoodmaps' creator (27 Aug), a YouTube explainer, and campus press (Daily Trojan, 16 Oct). No NYT, Guardian, Bloomberg CityLab, Verge, Wired or Fast Company coverage of the 2026 trend was found. The underlying observation is not new: analysts had drawn the same heatmap-equals-wealth/segregation conclusion in 2014 (Kansas City), 2015 (Florida), 2019 (Baltimore, Runner's World) and 2022 (Désir's *Running While Black*).

### Inferences
- The 2026 trend is a social-media re-discovery of a decade-old observation; what is new is (a) the explicit "use it to pick where to live / invest" framing, (b) quantitative follow-ups (Swiney's regression), and (c) product responses (Hoodmaps Income mode and crime overlays).
- The strongest spreaders were general-audience X accounts (5.3M + 1.9M + 1.2M views), not running media; mainstream newspapers appear not to have covered it as of mid-October 2026.
- Multiple reply threads immediately reframed "wealth" as "safety", and at least one account (@Hunter_Weiss) overlaid crime statistics, which signals that any product in this space will be read as a crime/safety map whether or not that is intended.

## Key Question 3: What existing websites, apps, datasets and academic projects already derive liveability/walkability/runnability-type signals from activity data?

### Takeaway
There is a mature ecosystem of *route-popularity* heatmaps (Strava Global/Night/Weekly/Personal, Garmin Trendline, RideWithGPS, Komoot, Suunto, Trailforks, AllTrails, Outdooractive), a planner-only aggregated dataset (Strava Metro, free to public agencies since 2020, ~3,500-4,000 partner organisations), a large academic literature using Strava for cycling-volume estimation and a smaller one relating heatmap intensity to street quality and housing prices, and a set of hobby proxies/scrapers for the heatmap tiles. No product was found that packages activity data into a consumer-facing neighborhood "runnability" or liveability score; the nearest consumer analogues are Walk Score (amenity-based) and Hoodmaps (crowd labels plus, since Aug 2026, census income).

### Cited Findings
**Strava consumer heatmaps**
- Global Heatmap: shows where users were active over the past year as "heat", updated monthly; no heat until "multiple users" have uploaded activities traversing an area; excludes activities not set to "Everyone", portions hidden by map-visibility settings, and users who opted out of aggregation — [Strava support](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15401880-the-global-heatmap-and-strava-metro)
- Origin: Strava published the Global Heatmap in Nov 2017 described as two years of trailing data (an earlier Strava Labs version existed from 2014 with ~77.7M rides and 19.7M runs) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strava); [Cooper Center](https://www.coopercenter.org/research/long-distance-runner-lonely-no-more); 2017 rebuild "based on 1 billion activities" — [road.cc](https://road.cc/content/news/231618-strava-launches-new-global-heatmap-based-1-billion-activities)
- Access history: since March 2018 only logged-in members can see the high-resolution heatmap (OSM forum); around Feb 2023 zoom levels above 12 began requiring authentication; since late March 2025 Strava deployed new tile URLs "with enhanced security features" that broke third-party extensions — [OSM help](https://help.openstreetmap.org/questions/62803/strava-heatmap-no-longer-useful-for-osm-mappers-anything-we-can-do); [OSM talk-au, Feb 2023](https://lists.openstreetmap.org/pipermail/talk-au/2023-February/016790.html); [OSM wiki](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Strava)
- Tiering as of 2026: Global Heatmap free (rolling ~12-13 months, monthly updates, Run/Ride/Water/Winter/Other filters); Personal, Night and Weekly heatmaps subscriber-only; heat appears only after at least 5 unique athletes contribute (per the5krunner) — [the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2026/01/16/strava-heatmaps-guide/); Weekly Heatmap "only available to Strava Subscribers", last 7 days, refreshed ~every 24 h — [Strava support: Weekly Heatmap](https://support.strava.com/hc/en-us/articles/26887067613197-Weekly-Heatmap); Night and Weekly heatmaps announced Nov/Dec 2024 — [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/strava-night-weekly-heatmap-safety-3507347/)
- Strava's own caveat (quoted by Domus): the heatmap "cannot be used to compare how many people are active in one place versus another" because it is normalised and not everyone uses the app — [Domus](https://www.domusweb.it/en/news/2026/09/02/strava-where-to-live-better-city-maps.html)
- Data access tightening (June 2026): Strava announced a flat $11.99/month fee for Standard-tier API access (new developers from 1 Jun 2026, existing from 30 Jun 2026), moved previously public pages (athlete profiles, club listings) behind authentication to stop scrapers, dropped support for third-party intermediary platforms, and said AI-firm scraping had "diminished and, in some cases, impaired" performance; 2024 API terms already prohibited using API data in AI models; Strava confidentially filed for an IPO on 2 Feb 2026 — [TechCrunch, 1 Jun 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/01/strava-declares-war-on-scrapers-ahead-of-ipo); [TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-strava-api-scraping-crackdown/)
- User base: Strava's 2 Feb 2026 press release claimed >180 million athletes in >185 countries; The Information estimated 150M+ (Jan 2026) and Sensor Tower ~50M monthly actives (2025); the 2024 Year in Sport cited 135M users — [Curved Trading summary](https://curvedtrading.com/articles/en/investing/strava-ipo/); [InsideHook](https://www.insidehook.com/wellness/strava-metro-became-secret-weapon-city-planners); ZME Science's "more than 200 million" figure was not confirmed by a primary source — [ZME Science](https://www.zmescience.com/science/news-science/stravas-heatmap-is-accidentally-becoming-a-map-of-where-people-want-to-live/)

**Strava Metro (planner dataset)**
- Launched May 2014; first customer Oregon DOT, which paid $20,000 for a one-year licence covering ~17,700 riders and ~400,000 bicycle trips; early clients London, Glasgow, Orlando, Alpine Shire (Australia) — [Planetizen, 2014](https://planetizen.com/node/68720); [FlowingData, 2014](https://flowingdata.com/2014/05/23/strava-metro-aims-to-help-cities-improve-biking-routes); [Fortune, 2014](https://fortune.com/2014/08/29/improve-urban-infrastructure-theres-an-app-for-that)
- Made free for urban planners, city governments and safe-infrastructure advocates in Sept 2020 (previously an annual fee since 2014) — [Bicycle Retailer, 23 Sep 2020](https://www.bicycleretailer.com/industry-news/2020/09/23/strava-metro-now-free-urban-planners-and-advocates)
- Scale: ~3,500 partner organisations (Dec 2024, InsideHook) / "over 4,000" (Velo-city 2026, Domus); Strava weekly activity ~2.5M rides/runs per week in 2014 vs ~40M per week in 2024; Metroview web platform with GIS export; "Metro data cannot be used for commercial purposes" (Tom Knights, Strava Metro) — [InsideHook, 6 Dec 2024](https://www.insidehook.com/wellness/strava-metro-became-secret-weapon-city-planners); [Velo-city](https://www.velo-city-conference.com/en/blog/strava-metro-helping-cities-deliver-the-urban-dream-with-data/); [Strava Metro](https://metro.strava.com/)
- Eligibility: organisations that plan, own or maintain active-transportation infrastructure; a separate academic access programme exists — [Inc.](https://www.inc.com/daniel-varghese/how-strava-helps-to-make-city-streets-safer-for-cyclists/91142785)
- Named city uses: Paris Plan Vélo 2021-2026 and Olympics planning; Milan Cambio Biciplan; TfL signal timing; Transport for NSW bushfire ridership; Belo Horizonte viaducts (+70% ridership in 18 months); LA County 2022 Parks Needs Assessment Plus; Pima County Loop pavement prioritisation; Denver Metroview demo — [InsideHook](https://www.insidehook.com/wellness/strava-metro-became-secret-weapon-city-planners)

### Inferences
- Everything consumer-facing today answers "where do people already run/ride?" (route popularity); nothing found answers "how good is this neighborhood for running/living?" as a score. That gap is the opening the Swiney/LinkedIn framing points at.
- The legitimate bulk-data path (Strava Metro) is explicitly non-commercial and restricted to agencies/academics; the consumer path (Global Heatmap tiles) is login-walled above zoom 12, hardened since March 2025, and Strava is actively hostile to scraping as of June 2026. A product built on scraped tiles would be fragile and likely violate Strava's terms (ToS not reviewed here).
- The academic literature validates Strava as a *relative* volume signal (correlates with counters) but not as a demographic or liveability measure.

### Gaps
- No public product named "Runnable" or any running-specific neighborhood scorer was found; the search for "runnability score" returned only walkability tools.
- Garmin, Komoot, Suunto and AllTrails pricing/openness details rest on the5krunner's comparison and Garmin forum posts, not primary pricing pages.
- Walk Score professional API prices and Redfin ownership were not confirmed in retrieved sources.
- Authors of the Boston 2023 paper and the two ScienceDirect bias studies could not be retrieved (403 / DNS failures).
- Whether labs.strava.com was formally retired is unconfirmed; the heatmap now lives at strava.com/maps.

## Key Question 4: Which of these are public and free, paid, or shut down?

### Takeaway
Free: Strava Global Heatmap (login needed above zoom 12), RideWithGPS community heatmap, Garmin Trendline (opt-in), Walk Score consumer site and 5,000-call/day API tier, SafeStreets, Hoodmaps. Paid/subscription: Strava Personal/Night/Weekly heatmaps, Strava API ($11.99/month from June 2026), Komoot/Trailforks/AllTrails/Outdooractive heatmaps, Walk Score professional tiers. Restricted-free: Strava Metro (free since 2020 but agencies/academics only, non-commercial). Shut down or withdrawn: Strava Metro's paid model (ended 2020), free unauthenticated high-zoom heatmap tiles (ended 2018/2023), legacy heatmap tile URLs used by OSM tooling (broken March 2025), free Standard-tier API access (ended June 2026).

### Inferences
- Every tightening of Strava access since 2018 has been triggered by misuse (military-base leak 2018, home-address inference 2023, scraping/AI 2025-26); a liveability product that visibly repurposes heatmap data is exactly the kind of use that provokes further restriction.
- The only durable, licensed route to area-level Strava data for a non-agency actor is an academic partnership via Metro, and even that forbids commercial use.

### Gaps

## Key Question 5: What critiques exist of using Strava data as a liveability proxy?

### Takeaway
Three independent critique strands converge: (1) demographic selection bias — Strava users are disproportionately male, 35-54, affluent, tech-proficient recreational cyclists, so heat measures who records rather than who lives there; (2) interpretive confounds — trails, parks, density, heat/shade and route-normalisation mean dark areas do not imply absence of activity or low quality; (3) privacy and security — the 2018 military-base exposure (Nathan Ruser, 13 trillion GPS points), the 2023 NC State home-address study (37.5% hit rate), the Oct 2024 Le Monde bodyguard investigation (Macron, Biden, Putin, Trump) and the July 2025 Dagens Nyheter Swedish PM/royals exposure (1,400+ workouts, 35+ location fixes). A fourth, equity strand (Désir, Williams & Behrendt, Daily Trojan) warns that treating heat as demand entrenches investment in already-served, whiter, wealthier areas.

### Cited Findings
**Demographic / selection bias**
- Williams & Behrendt, "Data visibility of cyclists: social justice implications of Strava Metro data in transport planning", *Mobilities* 21(2):381-394, March 2026 (online 2025), DOI 10.1080/17450101.2025.2542163: interviews with UK and US transport professionals; experts regard Strava Metro as statistically valid and consistent with counters yet "disproportionally capture journeys made by younger male cyclists"; warns that over-privileging highly visible cyclists distorts understanding and slows socially sustainable, diverse participation — [TRID record](https://trid.trb.org/View/2703964); [T&F](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17450101.2025.2542163)
- Urban-proximate protected areas study (2025, ScienceDirect): Strava Metro plus weather gives accurate daily trail-use estimates but is "not demographically representative, biased toward male cyclists aged 35-54"; ZME quotes the Strava sample as 93.5% male, 93.6% cyclists, 62.9% aged 35-54 vs 43.9% in the on-site survey — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2213078025001549); [ZME Science](https://www.zmescience.com/science/news-science/stravas-heatmap-is-accidentally-becoming-a-map-of-where-people-want-to-live/)
- "Bias and precision of crowdsourced recreational activity data from Strava", *Landscape and Urban Planning* (2023): Strava biased toward cyclists, males and middle age groups vs on-site observation; children and teenagers ~23.3% lower in Strava than observed — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0169204623000051)
- Literature review: Strava users "more likely to be younger, male, proficient in technology, and engaged in higher levels of physical activity"; women, children and older adults under-represented; recreational riders over-represented, commuters under-represented — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/343328882_Strava_Metro_data_for_bicycle_monitoring_a_literature_review)
- In 2014 Metro's data reflected "only a tiny percentage of riders"; Metro excludes private activities and opted-out users — [InsideHook](https://www.insidehook.com/wellness/strava-metro-became-secret-weapon-city-planners)
- Reply to the viral post: the map reflects "people who own smartwatches"; Bengaluru thread: "a map of rich people and tech corridors" — [ZME Science](https://www.zmescience.com/science/news-science/stravas-heatmap-is-accidentally-becoming-a-map-of-where-people-want-to-live/)

**Interpretive confounds**
- Strava: the heatmap cannot compare how many people are active in one place vs another; it is normalised and not everyone uses the app — [Domus](https://www.domusweb.it/en/news/2026/09/02/strava-where-to-live-better-city-maps.html)
- Swiney: busy trails (Monon, Riverside) inflate tract intensity without demographic change; "can't tell which story is which from just looking at the map"; R² only 0.25 — [Substack](https://cjacobswiney.substack.com/p/using-strava-to-underwrite-apartment)
- Daily Trojan: a dark patch "does not mean residents don't run there"; map cannot distinguish heat, dangerous crossings and fewer app users; LA heat disparities are predicted by income inequality — [Daily Trojan](https://dailytrojan.com/2026/10/16/stravas-heatmap-shows-us-where-running-in-la-is-possible/)
- ZME Science: "population density obviously matters too"; a dark area may reflect fewer recorded activities rather than absence of people — [ZME Science](https://www.zmescience.com/science/news-science/stravas-heatmap-is-accidentally-becoming-a-map-of-where-people-want-to-live/)
- 2026 preprint: mismatches between Strava and counters may stem from count-data bias too — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.00095)

**Equity / segregation critiques**
- Désir (2022): redlined Harlem/Brooklyn "nearly dark", Manhattan "lit up"; Baltimore "White L" vs "Black Butterfly" (Runner's World 2019) — [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/strava-heatmap-running-while-black-1849677409)
- Kansas City 2014 and Florida 2015 analyses reached the same conclusion visually — [Equitable Growth](https://equitablegrowth.org/astonishing-imprint-pattern-racial-housing-segregation-jogging-routes-kansas-city/); [Policy blog](https://policypolicy.wordpress.com/2015/05/13/strava-maps-urban-wealth-and-inequality/)
- Reply to the viral post notes realtors are legally barred from discussing crime and school quality, which is the context in which a "safety proxy" map would be used — [X post](https://x.com/NYCerInExile/status/2092008651288351088)

**Privacy and security incidents**
- Jan 2018: Nathan Ruser (Institute for United Conflict Analysts) showed the heatmap ("13 trillion GPS points") exposed US bases in Afghanistan and Syria, a suspected CIA site in Somalia, Area 51 and Russian bases; Strava said the map was "an aggregated and anonymised view of over a billion activities" and pledged to work with military officials — [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2018/01/29/strava-user-heatmap-reveals-patterns-of-life-in-western-military-bases/); [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2018/01/strava-heatmap-location-tracking.html); the 2018 exposure included a Royal Navy nuclear site and led to opt-out controls — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strava)
- June 2023: NC State "Heat marks the spot": collected public heatmaps for Arkansas, Ohio and North Carolina for a month, overlaid OSM residences, matched to voter rolls; ~37.5% of identified addresses correct; sparse areas are most exposed, dense areas protected by volume — [Malwarebytes](https://www.malwarebytes.com/blog/news/2023/06/strava-heatmap-loophole-may-reveal-users-home-addresses); [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/news/this-strava-feature-could-reveal-your-home-address-heres-how-to-turn-it-off)
- Oct 2024: Le Monde identified 26 US Secret Service agents, 12 French GSPR and 6 Russian FSO members with public Strava accounts; revealed Macron's private 2021 Honfleur weekend, the location of Putin's Sept 2023 meeting with Kim Jong Un in advance, and Biden's San Francisco hotel for the 2023 Xi talks; Secret Service: on-duty use banned but off-duty personal social media not prohibited; GSPR: issues "very slight" — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/10/29/biden-trump-harris-confidential-movements-fitness-app-strava-le-monde); [Computing](https://www.computing.co.uk/news/2024/security/world-leaders-locations-exposed-strava)
- July 2025: Dagens Nyheter reviewed 1,400+ workouts by seven Säpo bodyguards and mapped PM Ulf Kristersson's whereabouts at least 35 times, including his private home and an Åland hotel; royal family trips, Magdalena Andersson and Jimmie Åkesson also exposed; Strava: "there has not been a leak or breach"; Säpo investigating — [The Local](https://www.thelocal.se/20250708/sapo-bodyguards-strava-runs-reveal-locations-of-swedish-pm-and-royals); [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/world/europe/swedish-bodyguards-fitness-app-data-revealed-private-locations-royal-f-rcna217943); [Euronews](https://euronews.com/2025/07/09/swedish-pms-movements-leaked-by-bodyguards-uploading-workouts-on-strava)
- 2014 HN thread already noted faint hotspots at residential addresses reveal homes — [HN 7688378](https://home5.southeastasia.cloudapp.azure.com/item?id=7688378)

### Inferences
- A tract-level liveability product would inherit all three bias strands and could additionally be read as a redlining-adjacent "who lives here" map; the 2026 X discourse shows users converting wealth signal to crime/safety signal within hours.
- Privacy risk is highest exactly where heat is thinnest (sparse suburbs/rural), which is also where a "find undervalued neighborhoods" use case would look.
- Strava's pattern of reacting to each incident with access restrictions implies a reputational and platform-dependency risk for any third-party built on heatmap data.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study was found that tests heatmap intensity directly as a predictor of liveability, house-price change or gentrification in the US (the Chengdu paper is the closest; the Domus-cited Delmelle paper is unverified).
- Outcome of Säpo's 2025 investigation and any Strava product changes following Le Monde/DN were not found.
- Authors of the 2023 and 2025 ScienceDirect bias studies could not be retrieved (403).
